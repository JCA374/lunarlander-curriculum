# Episode 05: When the easy lessons do harm

This one is about a surprise that changes the whole plan. It turns out that the easy stages of the curriculum were not just useless. They were doing harm.

After the bug fix in the last episode, the new run started from the exact copy of the hand written pilot. That copy is good. On three hundred unseen flights in the full wind, it landed ninety percent of the time. The pilot itself lands ninety two. With the boulder in the way, the copy landed seventy one percent, and the pilot seventy seven. So NEAT started almost level with its teacher.

Then the curriculum began, as planned, with the easy stages. Calm air passed in five generations. The steady push passed after ninety one generations. On paper, two stages done.

But I've learned to check, so I flew those champions in the real task. After calm air, the full wind result had dropped from ninety to seventy nine percent. After the steady push, it was fifty two. With the boulder, from seventy one down to thirty eight. Every stage passed, and every stage made the lander worse at the thing we actually care about.

Here's why. Evolution only rewards what it sees. In the calm air stage, every flight was calm. So any random change that hurt the lander in wind cost nothing, and those changes piled up. The skill drifted away because nothing was protecting it. A curriculum is a great idea when you start from nothing. Easy lessons first, hard lessons later. But this network didn't start from nothing. It started as an expert copy. For an expert, the easy lessons are a holiday, and skills fade on holiday.

So I rebuilt the curriculum around what the network can't do yet. That's the boulder. The wind it already handles about as well as its teacher. The new curriculum has four stages. First, the boulder in calm air, starting at sixty percent of its size and growing to full size. Then the boulder in gusty wind. Then everything, with turbulence growing to full strength. And finally, the long polish towards ninety eight percent.

And there's one more rule, which I think is the important one. In every stage, four out of every ten training flights are the full, real task. Full wind, full turbulence, full size boulder. Teachers call it rehearsal. However easy the current lesson is, selection always keeps an eye on the real test. So a change that hurts the real task gets punished straight away, instead of quietly piling up.

The new run started a few minutes ago from the exact copy again. Its first generation landed ninety two percent of its training flights, a mix of calm air boulder flights and real ones.

I'll know within a couple of hours whether rehearsal keeps the skill. The thing to watch is simple. Does the lander get better at the real task while it learns the boulder, or at least not worse? If it does, this is the curriculum that can reach the goal.
