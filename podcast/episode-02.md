# Episode 02: Better than its teacher, and a lesson about forgetting

Welcome back. In the last episode, NEAT got its head start, a copy of a hand written pilot, and passed calm air in a few minutes. Since then, two things happened. One was a bug, and one was a surprise.

First, the bug. In the second stage, the lander faces a steady sideways push, like a constant wind from one side. The plan was to start the push gentle and make it stronger over the stage. But the push ignored that plan. It was always at full strength. On top of that, the training wheels were too firm. They are a gentle invisible hand that keeps the lander upright. I tested the hand written pilot with them. At full strength it landed a little less often than with no help at all, because the wheels stop the lander from tilting, and tilting is how it steers. With a light touch, about a third of the strength, it landed more often. So I stopped the first attempt, fixed the push, wrote a test for it, and turned the wheels down. Then I started again. Calm air passed immediately, in the very first generation.

Now the surprise. The steady push grew from gentle to full strength over about seventy generations. And then the champion passed the check. It landed ninety nine out of a hundred fresh flights, and ninety six on the confirmation. Then I compared it with its teacher, the hand written pilot, on the same three hundred unseen flights. Against a full strength steady push, the pilot landed about eighty two percent of the time. The evolved network landed about ninety seven percent. That is fifteen points better than the teacher, and the difference is clear, well outside the noise. For the first time in this project, evolution went past the pilot it started from.

But there is a catch, and it's an important one. I also flew the same champion in the real wind, the gusty kind, with turbulence. There it landed only sixty four percent of the time, while the pilot landed ninety two. So the network got very good at one kind of wind, and partly forgot the other. It specialised. That's a well known problem in curriculum learning, and now we have seen it here with real numbers.

Which is exactly why the next stage matters. It is running now. Real gusts, starting weak and growing to full strength, with turbulence switched off for the moment. The early generations look good, landing nearly every flight in gentle gusts. The question is whether it gets back to the pilot's level, and past it, once the gusts are at full strength. If it doesn't, the fix is clear. The steady push stage should be shorter, or mixed with real gusts, so the network never forgets them.

The run is doing about one generation every twenty five seconds. The gusts stage should reach full strength in roughly an hour. I'll check in every two hours, and the next episode comes when something passes or breaks.
